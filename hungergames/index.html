How the simulation works
  • Every game generates a new arena from a seed (type one in the box to replay a map). Terrain is built from layered noise into deep water, shallows, sand, scrub, meadow, forest, jungle, marsh, rock and peaks, with rivers running downhill from the highlands and lakes in the lowlands. Each arena has a theme, such as the Drowned Fen, Scorched Badlands, Iron Highlands, Emerald Jungle, Shattered Archipelago or Lakeland, that changes how much water, rock and woodland there is and how rich the resources are. The ground matters: jungle and forest hide people and slow them, marsh and deep water drag at them and make wounds fester, desert sand dries them out, and high ground sees farther. Wood grows in woods, stone on rock, fibre in meadow and reeds, berries along the meadow edges.
  • Tributes scavenge wood, stone, fiber and berries, then craft knives, axes, spears, bows, arrows and bandages. They loot bodies too.
  • Combat has windups, stamina, blocks and dodges, bleeding, kiting archers with real arrows in flight, and morale that breaks into flight.
  • Tributes talk, and mostly by asking questions: they ask for food, bandages, spare weapons and crafting materials (or where to find them), ask what others know, ask who is dangerous so they can be cautious, and ask where a revenge target went. They also propose alliances. Some lie, framing innocents or pretending to be allies while plotting betrayal. Liars get caught when a listener saw the truth.
  • Conversations have substance: witnesses describe where and when a kill happened and what weapon was used, listeners react (grief, doubt, vows of revenge), rumours get passed along second-hand, and tributes share small talk about their districts, the nights and the friends they have lost.
  • Injuries matter: hits land on the head, torso, arms or legs. Leg wounds slow a tribute and stop sprinting, arm wounds weaken and slow attacks (a broken arm rules out bows and axes), head blows cause concussion (stumbling, poor aim), and blood loss weakens, then drops a tribute to the ground. Open wounds can get infected until bandaged. Injured tributes rest, avoid fights they can’t win and heal slowly. Weapons can be parried, and a hit can interrupt a wind-up. Blood stains the ground (toggle it with the Blood button).
  • Romance: every tribute has an orientation (straight, gay / lesbian, bi / pan or ace), set at random and editable in Customize, and attraction follows it and each tribute’s pronouns (they/them and other non-binary tributes are drawn to the genders their orientation implies: straight = he or she, gay = other non-binary tributes). Friends who spend enough calm time together can develop a crush, then work up to a confession. It can be returned or turned down kindly. Couples stay close, defend each other fiercely, and are crushed when one dies. They can also break up if trust collapses or one betrays the other.
  • Tributes steal. Someone short on food, bandages, materials, arrows or a spare weapon may lift it from a non-ally standing nearby. Odds depend on skill and deceit, and improve at night, against someone busy or stunned, and hugely against a collapsed tribute. Get caught and the victim turns hostile; get away with it and the victim may only notice later. Bodies are the easy score: no roll, no witness grudge, and everything they carried (spare weapons included) is taken.
  • Tributes are solid: they steer around each other and cannot walk through one another. Use Customize to set names and pronouns.
  • Every tribute has a first and last name. Turn on Countries and each new game draws tributes from many different countries: names (first and last) are generated to match, the country shows as a flag in the tribute panel, and tributes sometimes mention where they are from and what home was like.
  • Only people who see a kill know who did it. Finding a body tells you someone died, not who did it. Witnesses to a friend’s death swear revenge.
  • Tributes also think. Thoughts float above them as faint, dashed, italic bubbles: doubt about a rumour (“that can’t be true, i saw…”), grief, fear, plans. Bubbles stack upward as more is said instead of replacing each other, and fallen tributes stay on the map, greyed out.
  • Every night the anthem plays and the faces of everyone who fell since the last night are shown in the sky: an announcement in the arena and the event log. Friends of the fallen grieve, and killers notice their victims on display.
  • Hope: every tribute has a hope stat that follows their morale, injuries, hunger, thirst and whether they have allies or a partner. If hope and morale both collapse and the situation is dire (badly hurt, starving, or utterly alone), a tribute near the storm wall or a firestorm may give up. A friend nearby can talk them out of it, and an attempt can fail, leaving them badly hurt. Turn this off with the Despair button.
  • Stalemates break. When four or fewer remain and nobody has died for a while, a tribute who truly wants it (aggressive, deceitful, starving or cornered by the storm) may betray an ally or stop waiting and go after someone. It is rare, and partners only turn on each other after a very long standoff. If the last few still won’t move, the safe circle slowly shrinks further.
  • Personalities and grief: every tribute has a personality (kind, friendly, silly, neutral, cold, competitive, shy or stoic) that shifts how social or aggressive they are. When a partner, crush, friend or district partner dies, tributes grieve in proportion to how close they were and how they handle loss: they stop to mourn, lose morale and hope, fight worse and say so aloud. Cold tributes barely flinch and competitive ones turn it into resolve. A grieving tribute is vulnerable: some rivals see an easy target, while kind and friendly tributes (and allies) come to comfort them, which speeds their recovery.
  • Layout: the Layout button switches between the boxed view and a full-screen view on wide screens. Full screen fills the window with the arena in the middle, the event log on the left as a live chat (newest at the bottom, scroll up to read back and it stays put), and the selected tribute and tribute list on the right. Your choice is remembered.
  • Conversations have depth and variety. Every tribute has a hidden background (family, a fear, a wish, a hobby, a regret, a favourite memory) shown in their panel. Friends and allies open up about it, the other often opens up in return, and later they remember what was said and ask about it. Beyond asking for help and swapping rumours, tributes joke (it only lands if the listener is the type to laugh), share food unprompted, compliment each other, flirt, make plans with allies (including quiet talks about what happens when it comes down to just them), and pick fights with people they dislike. How each line is phrased depends on personality, and greetings and goodbyes change with how well they know each other.
  • Gamemakers drop gifts, ignite firestorms and close a storm wall. Last tribute standing wins.

Customize tributes

Edit first names, last names and pronouns. Changes apply to this game right away and are remembered in this browser for future games. Pronouns: she/her, he/him, they/them, it/its, or your own such as xe/xem/xyr.
